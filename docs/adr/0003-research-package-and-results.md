# ADR 0003: A research package, and results as a generated document

Date: 2026-11-08 · Status: Accepted

## Context

A review in November 2026 backtested the committee's own deterministic
rules on twenty years of daily data and found they lost to cash since
2022 and to a trend-filtered ETF portfolio in every window. It also
tested an LSTM baseline (less accurate than "always up") and the three
best-documented intraday rules on ten years of 5-minute bars. The scripts
that produced those numbers were throwaway: untested, unreviewed, and
not reproducible by anyone else.

## Decision

Turn the throwaway code into `midas/research`: typed, tested, and held to
the strict mypy profile. The package has a data layer with a local cache,
a drift-between-rebalances simulator with per-side costs, strategy
functions, the swing-committee clone, intraday rules, a walk-forward
selector and a report builder. Conventions are fixed in one place:
signals on a close trade at the next open, cash earns the T-bill yield,
Sharpe is excess over cash.

`docs/results.md` is generated by `midas research report` and never
edited by hand. The port was checked against the review's output: CAGR
and Sharpe agree to four decimals for every strategy, with one deliberate
change (GEM's absolute-momentum gate follows Antonacci exactly).

Tests use synthetic series with known answers (a constant-weight
portfolio reproduces the asset, a rebalance pays exactly its turnover
cost, a hand-built session produces a known R multiple), not snapshots of
real data.

## Consequences

Any claim in the README about what works and what does not points at a
table a reader can regenerate. Strategy code and backtest code are the
same functions (`midas.strategy.core` trades `tactical_sma`'s rule), so
simulation and production cannot quietly diverge.

## Alternatives considered

- A notebook: readable, but unreviewable and untested.
- An external backtesting framework: more features, but the point was to
  own and test the exact conventions the live book uses.
